# SEO, GEO and AI search questions: what a strong answer covers

Use these as the checklist for each outline. The candidate's own story fills the S, T and R; this file only says what the A should prove. If the candidate has no story, the method below is the answer.

## SEO vs GEO vs AEO

SEO gets a page ranked. GEO and AEO get it quoted or cited inside an AI answer (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode). The groundwork overlaps heavily: crawlable, indexed, fast, clearly structured pages. What's new is the measurement (citation rates per engine, run many times) and writing pages to be a source as well as a result. A strong answer doesn't sell a new label.

## How do you measure AI visibility?

The question they'll judge the candidate on. **Rate, not rank.**

- A fixed, written set of buyer questions, from Search Console queries, People Also Ask and real customer wording. No brand-name questions in the core set.
- Each question run many times per engine, on more than one day. One answer is an anecdote.
- Report appearance rate and citation rate, **each engine on its own**, never one blended score.
- A baseline and a dated window before any change, so a lift can be put down to the work.
- First-party numbers beside it: AI Overviews / AI Mode impressions in Search Console, AI referral sessions in GA4. In separate rows from Google organic.

Best anchored in a real study the candidate ran, even a small one.

## Show us an AI workflow you built and how you checked it

What proves it: the check was designed **before** the first run (an answer key, a rubric, a hash of the expected results), the conditions were held fixed (same prompt, same model, fresh environment per run), there was a baseline (the plain tool with no add-ons), and grading was blind or done by two graders. Bring a screen to share. A production workflow works too if a person approves anything that publishes, sends or spends.

## Have you deployed pages or code with AI coding agents yourself?

Name the stack, what the agent wrote, what the candidate decided and steered, and one concrete change with a before and after number (a speed score, a build check that blocks bad posts). Say how changes get reviewed.

## What do you change on a site to get cited?

Three levers, in order: structured data in templates (one fix covers every page; engineering's part), entity coverage (products, people, places named the way people ask), answer-first content with a quotable sentence and a number worth citing. Then the step most audits skip: check who gets cited today. For service and "best X" questions it's mostly directories and listicles, so part of the work is off-site.

## A founder wants a guarantee they'll show up in ChatGPT

No guarantee: nobody controls what a model picks, and the same question twice can return different lists. Say what the candidate does control (being the easiest source to read, check and quote) and how progress is shown (baseline citation rate per engine, re-run monthly).

## Tell me about a time AI got something wrong

A real moment where an AI draft or output contained something false, how it was caught, and **what changed in the setup afterwards** (a rule, a check, an approval step). The change is the answer.

## How do you prove SEO caused a lift?

Baseline and dated window before each change. Did the lift start inside the window, and only on the pages that changed? Compare against untouched pages. Mark Google updates on the timeline. When cause can't be separated, say so in the report.

## How do you keep AI-assisted content from reading as AI?

Detector scores disagree with each other and are a poor target. What works: a voice file built from the writer's own edits, real moments and first-party numbers, a source behind every claim, and an opinion the writer would defend.

## Clicks dropped but rankings held

Method question. Pull 16 months of Search Console page by page, find pages that kept position but lost CTR, check those queries by hand for AI Overviews, AI Mode or new SERP features. Fix what gives a reason to click: title and meta written against the AI answer, a clear answer in the first lines so the page can be the cited source. Track page-level clicks for 28 days against the baseline.

## llms.txt, FAQ schema, or question headings for AI?

Check the current Google guidance on the day. As of July 2026, Google's guide to generative AI features in Search said Search doesn't use llms.txt, there's no special schema for AI, and content doesn't need to be chunked or rewritten for AI; FAQ rich results had stopped showing in May 2026. Keep schema that describes the page truthfully. First check where answers for the queries come from today.

## One sprint, 40-item backlog

Score on reach (pages or revenue touched), effect and effort. Template-level fixes first: indexing blockers, schema, canonicals, redirects. Anything blocking crawl or indexing beats anything that only improves a page. Content fixes go to the content team in parallel. Every ticket says what done looks like and how it'll be measured.
